BITS • QUBITS • INFORMATION SCIENCE

The information foundation:
bits & qubits.

InTeL treats classical and quantum information as related but distinct engineering domains, with the four pillars implemented using mechanisms appropriate to each.

Classical digital information — bits

A bit is the basic unit of classical digital information and is represented as either 0 or 1. Bits underpin conventional computers, networks, storage systems, software, sensors and digital services. Bit-level engineering includes encoding, compression, transmission, storage, access control, encryption, processing, validation and presentation.

Quantum information — qubits

A qubit is a quantum-information unit prepared in a quantum state. Unlike a classical bit, it can occupy a superposition of basis states and may participate in entanglement with other quantum systems. Quantum information is manipulated through quantum operations and measured using quantum measurement processes.

The bit–qubit relationship

Classical computers remain essential for user interfaces, orchestration, control, conventional data management, error handling and most everyday workloads. Quantum processors and quantum communication systems may be integrated for selected tasks when they provide a measurable benefit.

DimensionBit-level informationQubit-level information
RepresentationClassical binary value: 0 or 1Quantum state; may involve superposition of basis states
OperationsBoolean and classical logic operationsQuantum gates and controlled quantum evolution
ObservationDigital values can generally be read and copied within system rulesMeasurement yields outcomes and can disturb the quantum state
CopyingDigital data can be duplicated subject to permissions and integrity controlsUnknown quantum states cannot be perfectly cloned
SecurityEncryption, authentication, access control and integrityQuantum protocol security, device trust, implementation security and post-quantum migration
Engineering constraintsLatency, bandwidth, storage, energy, reliability and software correctnessNoise, decoherence, measurement, limited scale and error correction
InTeL design rule

The four pillars apply at both bit and qubit levels, but mechanisms differ. Quantum communication, quantum computing and quantum security are not interchangeable, and quantum technology does not automatically make a system secure or faster.

Engineering implications

A sound architecture identifies which information is classical and which is quantum; defines conversion and measurement boundaries; documents assumptions about hardware and channels; uses classical control where required; and validates any claimed advantage against strong classical alternatives.
