C-InTeL — Communication & Connectivity

Improves the movement of information across networks, devices and communities through interoperable communications, resilient connectivity and efficient protocols.

Domain purpose

Improves the movement of information across networks, devices and communities through interoperable communications, resilient connectivity and efficient protocols. This domain is part of the integrated InTeL portfolio and is not intended to operate as an isolated product. Its scope is defined by the information problems, users, systems and operating conditions it serves.

Four-pillar implementation

Information Transfer enables exchange; Information Security protects assets and trust; Information Manifestation makes outputs interpretable; Information Utilization converts information into domain-specific outcomes.

PillarDomain implementation focus
Information TransferIdentify sources, destinations, interfaces, formats, timing, synchronization and delivery assurance relevant to C-InTeL.
Information SecurityDefine assets, identities, permissions, privacy requirements, threat models, integrity checks, audit evidence and recovery controls.
Information ManifestationRepresent source information faithfully through records, interfaces, visualizations, models, signals or authorized outputs.
Information UtilizationApply validated information to support knowledge, workflows, decisions, services and measurable domain outcomes.

Three technology pathways

V1 Digital: use mature digital systems, software, networks and AI. V2 Digital–Quantum: integrate a selected quantum component only when a credible use case and measurable benefit exist. V3 Quantum: explore quantum-first research only where the domain problem is scientifically suitable. Classical orchestration, control, security and interpretation remain important.

Typical solution lifecycle

Define the problem and users; map information assets and flows; establish the four pillar requirements; select the least complex suitable technology; prototype and test; validate security, safety and performance; deploy only with accountable ownership; monitor outcomes and improve.

Measures of success

Set baselines and targets for delivery reliability, latency, interoperability, security and privacy findings, representation accuracy, accessibility, decision usefulness, adoption, cost-benefit and reproducibility. Quantum-enabled experiments must additionally report device characteristics, noise/error conditions, calibration, repeatability, classical baselines and measurement limits.
