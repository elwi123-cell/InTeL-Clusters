INTEGRATED INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

One lifecycle.
Multiple technology layers.

The shared architecture that connects information foundations, transfer, trust, representation, intelligence, applications and integration.

Integrated information lifecycle

InTeL uses a six-stage lifecycle: create or acquire information; transfer it; secure and verify it; manifest it into interpretable forms; utilize it; then assure, measure and improve the system. Feedback from utilization may create new information and restart the cycle.

StageCore activityBit-level examplesQubit-level examples
1. Create / acquireGenerate, sense or receive informationSensors, user input, files, databases, digital recordsQuantum state preparation, quantum sensing, quantum device output
2. TransferMove information between endpointsNetworks, APIs, storage replication, secure messagingQuantum channels, state transfer protocols, entanglement distribution with classical coordination
3. SecureProtect and verify informationEncryption, authentication, authorization, integrity, backupsProtocol security, device trust, implementation assurance, classical interface protection
4. ManifestRepresent and interpret informationDecode, render, visualize, model, publish, displayMeasure, characterize, interpret outcomes and create classical representations
5. UtilizeAnalyse and apply informationAI, analytics, software, automation, decisionsHybrid algorithms, quantum simulation, specialized quantum processing and classical analysis
6. Assure / improveMeasure results, learn and correctMonitoring, audit, incident response, quality improvementCalibration, noise/error analysis, reproducibility and benchmark comparison

Cross-cutting architecture layers

The information foundation comprises bits, qubits, hardware, processors, sensors, networks, storage and compute. Above it sit transfer, security and trust, manifestation, utilization and intelligence, applications, and integration. Z-InTeL coordinates shared interfaces, cross-domain workflows and assurance.

Architecture design rule

Select the least complex method that meets performance, security, safety and cost requirements. Security is cross-cutting; the lifecycle may repeat or run in parallel. Classical systems remain essential to control, orchestrate and interpret many quantum workflows.
